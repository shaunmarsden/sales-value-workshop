# Vantree Value Workshop Output

> This is a fictional example. Vantree, the people and the figures are invented.

## The Work Today

Vantree Manufacturing is a mid-market industrial parts maker. Custom order quotes currently pass between sales, the estimating team and operations before a price goes back to the customer. Delays and inconsistent pricing have been noticed, but during the workshop it became clear that no single person or team is actually responsible for how the process runs end to end. Sales blames estimating for slow turnaround, estimating says operations changes specifications late, and operations says they are never told about deadlines.

## Why It Matters

- Quotes sometimes take over a week, with nobody able to explain why in a specific case
- Customers occasionally receive different prices for similar requests
- Nobody in the room could describe the current process from end to end without disagreement

## Better Looks Like

The group could describe roughly what better would feel like: faster, more consistent quoting. It could not agree who would judge whether a change actually helped, since no one owns the whole process today.

## First Test

Not proposed. The [Value Evidence Check](../methods/value-evidence-check.md)'s Ownership row names exactly this gap: nobody could explain who does the work today, and the process spans three teams with no single owner. Proposing a test now would mean guessing at an owner who does not yet exist.

## Decision

Vantree agreed to first name a single owner for the quoting process end to end, something the workshop itself could not manufacture. No test, tool or measurement was agreed. This is an honest "not yet".

## What Remains Unknown

- Who would actually own this process if someone were named
- Whether the delay sits mostly in estimating, operations, or the handoff between them
- Whether a measured baseline is even possible without an owner to gather it
