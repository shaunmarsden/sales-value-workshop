# Run a First Workshop

You do not need a large room, a slide deck or a finished solution. One useful conversation is enough to establish whether there is a problem worth exploring.

## Before the Conversation

Bring one concrete area of work, not "AI transformation" as a whole. Ask the customer to bring the people who understand the work and the people who would judge whether a change helped.

Make the boundary clear:

> We are here to understand one piece of work and decide whether a small test is useful. We are not here to agree a technology purchase.

## Start With the Work

Ask:

1. What is happening today, step by step?
2. Which part creates the most delay, rework, uncertainty or frustration?
3. Who feels that problem and who owns it?
4. What evidence would show that the problem is real?

Write down the exact examples people give. Do not turn "it is a pain" into a claim about hours or money without checking.

## Define Better

Ask:

1. If this was working better in three months, what would people notice?
2. What would be easier, quicker, safer or more consistent?
3. Which measure would be useful enough to track?
4. What would make the change not worth continuing?

Good measures can be simple: time spent on one task, rework created, follow up completed, quality checks passed or confidence in a handover. A useful measure is better than an impressive one.

## Choose a First Test

Keep it small:

- one team or a small group of users;
- one type of task;
- approved information only;
- a named person checking the output;
- a clear start and review date; and
- no automatic customer messages or system changes.

Ask, "What would this small test need to show for you to take the next conversation seriously?"

## Close Well

Read back:

- the problem as the customer described it;
- what remains unknown;
- the proposed test, if one was agreed;
- who owns each action; and
- what you will review next.

If the evidence does not support a test, say so. A useful workshop can end with "not yet".
